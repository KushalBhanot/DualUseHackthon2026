# Night Ledger: What the Supervisor Decides

## Who

The **overnight house supervisor**. At night they are often the most senior person in the building, with one other person on the phones, or a security guard covering the switchboard.

## What changes for them

The AI does the sorting. Every caller is screened for red flags and placed in a lane before any human time is spent. Red-flag calls go straight to a nurse without waiting on the supervisor.

The supervisor no longer decides **which ringing phone to pick up next**. They decide only what must stay with a person, and the system brings each decision to them with the evidence attached.

## The decisions

| # | Decision | When it comes up | What the AI gives them |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Switch on surge mode?** | Call volume jumps far above normal for the hour | "18 calls in 3 min (normal ≈ 1). 5 about the outage or ER. 18 on hold. 0 red flags waiting." Buttons: *Switch on surge mode* / *Not yet, ask in 2 min* |
| 2 | **Play a status message to every caller?** | One question dominates the line | A pre-written message ready to approve, e.g. "The ER is open on backup power" |
| 3 | **Who in the A person soon queue gets called back first?** | More amber callers than free hands | A ranked queue with reasons: child under 2, confused caller, family looking for someone |
| 4 | **What to do when someone doesn't pick up?** | An amber caller misses two callbacks | An alert at the top of the queue. The supervisor chooses: try again, ask police for a welfare check, or close it |
| 5 | **Lower a caller's lane?** | They think a caller was over-triaged | A confirmation step. Only a person can lower a lane |
| 6 | **Call in more staff or declare an incident?** | Red-flag calls or the callback backlog keep growing | Numbers on screen. The AI recommends but never declares |

## The moment

**2:10 AM, four minutes after an earthquake knocks out power.** Calls arrive every few seconds. Without surge mode, every non-urgent caller waits on hold in arrival order, and they start hanging up.

The key decision is **#1 plus #2 together**: keep answering one by one, or switch to surge mode and play the status message.

- **Made in the first five minutes:** held callers get callback times instead of waiting, the outage callers get their answer from the status message, and the line stays usable.
- **Made at minute 40:** the supervisor is only reacting to a line that has already flooded.

If the supervisor picks *Not yet*, the system asks again two minutes later with updated numbers, including how many callers hung up in the meantime. Switching on also schedules callbacks for callers who already hung up, since their number is known.

## Pre-emptive, not reactionary

The real preparation happens **on a calm day**:

- The red-flag questions and lane rules are agreed with nursing leadership in advance.
- Status-message templates for likely events (power outage, fire, earthquake, IT outage) are written and approved ahead of time.
- The escalation list (who gets the call if the nurse doesn't answer) is set.

At 2 AM the supervisor approves rather than writes. Each decision is one confirmed click.

## What the supervisor never has to do

- Decide whether a chest-pain caller should wait. Red flags skip the supervisor and go straight to a nurse.
- Repeat the basics. Every escalated call arrives with a Handoff Card: who, which red flags, callback number, transcript.
- Trust the AI to lower urgency. It can't. Only a person can lower a lane.
