Preparing for Disaster: The Reveal Page 
MYSTERY PROMPT @ Friday 6.15 PM

KICK OFF DECK
As automation takes centre stage in the systems we depend on, more of them act on their own, and sometimes they stop behaving as expected. Something is where it shouldn't be, doing what nobody authorized, or has gone quiet when it should be talking. It could be a vehicle, a grid, a phone line, a shipment, a hospital ward. 
The usual answer is a bang: shut it down, cut it off, call everyone. A bang works, and it often costs more than the problem.
Your challenge: Help someone prepare for disaster, how can you be pre-emptive rather than reactionary? Pick any person who has to decide, and find the gentlest response that still works: cheap enough to use widely, safe around people, and useful on an ordinary day too. This is decision making with a dual-use twist. 
We won't tell you what to build. Three times this weekend, we'll tell you the problem has changed with twists, you are encouraged (but not required) to incorporate them.

NEW TO "DUAL-USE"? THIRTY SECONDS.
A dual-use tool leads two lives. On an ordinary day it earns its keep, so people keep it around. In a crisis it matters, and it is already in place. It serves everyday people and people in a hurry, such as responders, operators and defenders.
GPS began as military navigation and now sits in every phone.
The microwave oven grew out of radar.
Android phones use their motion sensors to spot earthquakes and warn people nearby.
Here it does not mean weapons. It means a tool worth owning before the emergency, so that it is there when the emergency comes.

HOW TO START
Between doing nothing and the bang there is a ladder: watch, ask, slow, hold, hand to a person. Your build lives on one or two rungs. Four choices make a project:
A person who has to decide.
A moment when they have to decide it. Use the Harbor Night in the Extended Card, or your own.
A second customer who would use it on an ordinary Tuesday.

THE TRACKS
Four tracks, one night. 
IBM SkillsBuild is open to every track. Build with IBM Bob and compete for Best Build with IBM Bob.
01. Marine, with NULL Systems: Under the Water
The seat: the harbor watch officer. An unauthorized autonomous vehicle is in a working harbor. Detecting small underwater vehicles is getting easier. Handling one safely is not. What sits between "seen" and "handled"? What should a small, low-cost platform decide alone, and what must a person approve? What does it do for a living on a quiet Tuesday, like finding a lost research vehicle? Cost, safety and scale all count. Elegant beats loud. Your work stops at the handoff to a human. How a vehicle is physically halted is out of scope this weekend.
02. Energy, with Base Power: On the Grid
The seat: the dispatcher of a home-battery fleet. Homes full of automated batteries are now part of the grid, and the harbor, the phones and the cold rooms all run on it. When something strains or goes odd, the blunt move is shedding load. What sits between "normal" and "shed"? Whose power can wait, and how would you know? When a battery goes silent, is it broken, tripped or just not talking? How does the same battery earn its keep on an ordinary day?
03. Communication, with Retell AI: On the Line
The seat: the overnight supervisor of a call line. A surge of calls and alerts no one can fully verify, and too few people to answer them. Residents are your earliest sensor and your biggest flood. The blunt move is to alert everyone, or send everything to a human who isn't there. How do you tell who needs a person right now? What must never be automated? What happens when someone doesn't pick up?
04. Ports, with Terminal49: At the Gate
The seat: the terminal planner. One box, four stories. The carrier says it's empty, the terminal scale says it's heavy, customs has a hold on it, and the refrigeration plug says it's warming. A hundred more boxes need the same gate, half the lanes are down, and the clock on charges is running. The blunt move is to hold everything or close the terminal. What can you slow, reroute, inspect or release? Which source do you trust? Which box can't wait?


HOW IT IS JUDGED
The five criteria on the site, 1 to 5 each:dual-use fit, technical execution, feasibility, impact, adapting to twists, demo and/or pitch. 
HINT: The easiest way to show dual-use fit is to name your second customer and the day they would use it.
